FRSC recruitment should be treated as free unless an official notice clearly states otherwise through the proper public channel. In most scam situations, payment demands are among the strongest warning signs that a candidate may be dealing with false information.

When money enters the conversation, caution should increase immediately.

## Why this question matters so much

Scammers often tell candidates they must pay for:

- application access
- shortlist placement
- screening confirmation
- special processing
- guaranteed success

These claims are usually designed to create fear and urgency.

## What is the safer rule?

If you receive a payment request through a personal phone number, unofficial link, private chat, or suspicious message, do not treat it as legitimate. Go back to the official FRSC source and verify before you do anything.

That pause can save money and stress.

## Why panic makes candidates vulnerable

Many people pay because they fear missing an opportunity. That emotional pressure is exactly what scammers use. A calmer verification habit is one of the best protections a candidate can build.
