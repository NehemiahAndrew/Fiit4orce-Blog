To check if Federal Fire Service recruitment is official, verify the update from a real public source before you click a link, share personal information, or respond to payment instructions. Fake recruitment information often looks polished, urgent, and convincing, but appearance is not proof.

Verification should always come first.

## What does official recruitment information usually look like?

Official information should connect candidates to a real public process, recognized instructions, and a source that does not depend on private negotiation. It should not ask you to trust a stranger, pay unofficial fees, or act in panic.

When reviewing a message, ask:

- does it point to a recognized source?
- does it match a public recruitment process?
- is anyone asking for money or special help?
- does the link seem suspicious?

If something feels hidden or rushed, pause and verify.

## What are common scam warning signs?

Be careful when a message:

- promises guaranteed selection
- asks for processing fees through private channels
- creates panic so you act quickly
- directs you to a suspicious form
- tells you to ignore official instructions

These are classic recruitment scam patterns.
