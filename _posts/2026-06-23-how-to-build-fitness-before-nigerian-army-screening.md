If you want to build fitness before Nigerian Army screening, the most effective plan is usually a steady one. Many candidates make the mistake of waiting too long, then trying to force results with extreme exercise. That approach often leads to fatigue, inconsistency, or injury rather than real readiness.

Good screening fitness is less about dramatic effort and more about discipline over time.

## Start with consistency, not intensity

The first goal should be to train regularly enough that your body adapts well. When candidates jump from inactivity into hard workouts, they often become sore, discouraged, or irregular.

Instead, build around a routine you can repeat.

That usually means:

- training several times each week
- increasing effort gradually
- giving your body time to recover
- focusing on sustainable improvement

Consistency creates the base that intensity can build on later.

## Build stamina first

A lot of candidates benefit from improving endurance early. Better stamina helps you handle physical activity, waiting, movement, and the general pressure of recruitment preparation.

Good stamina work can include:

- brisk walking
- jogging
- short run intervals
- regular cardio sessions

The aim is to become more comfortable with sustained effort, not to exhaust yourself every session.

## Add bodyweight strength work

Basic strength matters too. Bodyweight training is often a practical starting point because it builds control, discipline, and functional readiness without requiring special equipment.

A simple approach can include:

- push-up progressions
- squats
- planks
- lunges
- mobility drills

Start from your current level and improve steadily instead of comparing yourself to others.

## Protect recovery and sleep

Fitness improvement does not happen only during exercise. Recovery matters. Candidates who ignore sleep, hydration, and rest often perform worse even when they feel like they are training hard.

Support your fitness work with:

- regular sleep
- hydration
- rest between demanding sessions
- realistic progression instead of daily overexertion

Training harder is not always the same as training better.

## Avoid the last-minute rush

Last-minute training plans are one of the biggest mistakes candidates make. If you only become serious a few days before screening, you are more likely to feel tired than ready.

A better plan is to begin early, stay steady, and keep building from there.
